# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Teams in Pakistan who meet in person, around one table, and talk in a natural mix of English and Urdu: project teams, student groups, small companies. Everyone in the room has the app on their phone and sets it down in front of them. They use it before a meeting (join, consent, check who is there), during it (glance at a timer and a signal that recording is healthy), and after it (find what was decided and who owns what). Most use is on mid-range Android phones, sometimes on slow mobile data.

The job: stop meetings from evaporating. Get a trustworthy record of who said what and what was decided, without anyone taking notes.

## Product Purpose

An English-Urdu meeting intelligence app for in-person, multi-speaker rooms. Each phone records its own track. Tracks are aligned, speech is attributed to named people by enrolled voiceprints, and the result becomes a searchable memory of decisions and action items, with an assistant that can act on them. Existing tools assume a virtual call and English only; this one serves a room of people talking over each other.

Success is a team that stops asking "what did we agree last time?" because the app already answers, with a citation back to the exact moment. It is also a final-year project, so every screen has to be explainable by the three students who built it.

## Brand Personality

A piece of audio hardware you trust with your voice: calm, exact, a little tactile. Three words: instrument, precise, trustworthy. It speaks plainly, in sentence case, without exclamation marks. It is bilingual by default, not as a translation layer, so Urdu text is typeset with the same care as English.

## Anti-references

- The generic "AI product" look: purple-to-blue gradients, glowing orbs, sparkle icons.
- Dense enterprise dashboards with heavy boxes around everything.
- Gamified or playful tools that make recording people feel casual. This handles consent and voiceprints.
- Glassmorphism and gradients used as decoration. Warm paper tones are used on purpose, as a recorder's notebook, not as a generic editorial default.
- Apps where Urdu is an afterthought: clipped Nastaliq, left-aligned RTL, mismatched fonts.

Closest in spirit: Teenage Engineering's TP-7 recorder for monochrome surfaces, mono labels and one signal colour; Granola for the warm paper canvas and serif headlines on reading screens; Just Press Record for the big bottom-centre record button.

## Design Principles

1. **Neutrals do the work.** One orange signal colour, and it means recording or live and nothing else.
2. **Say what is happening.** Recording, uploading and processing states are always visible and always in words as well as color.
3. **Two scripts, equal care.** Each transcript segment is set in the font and direction of its own language.
4. **Consent is a first-class screen.** Never buried, never rushed, never decorative.
5. **Never blank.** Every screen has a considered loading, empty and error state.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA contrast in both themes (4.5:1 body text, 3:1 large text and UI). Touch targets of at least 44pt. Status never relies on color alone. Reduced-motion users get instant or crossfade transitions. Dynamic type should not break layouts. Right-to-left text must align and wrap correctly, with Urdu line height tall enough that Nastaliq is never clipped. "Guest" (a named outside attendee) and "Unknown Speaker N" (an unmatched transcript segment) are different things and are never interchanged in copy.
